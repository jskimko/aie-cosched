When one in-order command processor feeds all the engines of a spatial NPU, the order compiled into a design is the schedule that the array runs, and how the design cuts its work and the prompt length decide how much rescheduling recovers, as a condition read from the artifacts predicts.
On AIR's fused designs for five LLMs, OPCORD's post-compilation rewrites of issue order and buffers behind legality checkers shorten both prefill and decode with bit-identical outputs, its reading of compiled designs explains why the flash prefill and decode of a closed engine's current release leave little to recover, and its event model chooses the order and the configuration together for each prompt length.
Other accelerators in which one in-order issuer feeds spatially partitioned engines may face the same choice.
The speedups and the stack comparisons are snapshots of current releases, whereas the insight and the condition hold as designs improve, and each improved design is a new input to OPCORD rather than a replacement for it.
