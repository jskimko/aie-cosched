\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

% VALUE: one setup paragraph: the platform and its power state, the software, the workloads and prompt lengths, the baseline, how TTFT is measured, the correctness gate, and how the machine state is controlled; defaults stated once
We measure on a GMKtec NucBox EVO-X2 with an AMD Ryzen\texttrademark{} AI Max+ 395 processor, whose XDNA~2 NPU has 8 columns of 4 compute tiles, and 64\,GB of shared system memory.
The host runs Linux~7.0 with the amdxdna driver and XRT~2.25.0~\cite{xdna-driver,xrt-repo}, the NPU in its performance power mode, and the CPU's frequency governor and energy preference set to performance.
We evaluate prefill of Llama-3.2-1B~\cite{meta2024llama32} and Qwen3-4B~\cite{yang2025qwen3} with 4-bit quantized weights in FastFlowLM's format~\cite{fastflowlm-repo}, at prompts of 512, 2{,}048, and 4{,}096 tokens.
The designs are MLIR-AIR's fused prefill~\cite{wang2026mlirair}, compiled with MLIR-AIE~\cite{mlir-aie-repo} and used unchanged.
The baseline is each design's own host program, which runs the same configuration, kernels, and weights as our composed schedules.
Every run's logits must be bit-identical to the baseline's, and the kernel log must show no driver or IOMMU message during the run.
TTFT is the wall time of the prefill call, from token identifiers to logits, and we report the median of 3 timed runs after one warm run, with the order of schedules rotated between rounds.
Each batch of runs holds the device exclusively, checks that no other process has it open, and brackets the runs with a DRAM bandwidth probe and an empty-command dispatch.
Because the aggregate DRAM read cap varies by up to 11\% between batches, every ratio compares runs within one batch.
