\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

% VALUE: the platform and its power state, the software, the designs, and the five LLMs (three classes of edge LLM)

Every run's logits and KV records are bit-identical to the original program's, and the kernel log stays free of driver and IOMMU messages.

\subsection{End-to-End Speedup}
\label{subsec:e2e}

% VALUE: OPCORD shortens TTFT on every LLM from 1K tokens, against the baseline, with bit-identical outputs; the gain rises with the prompt length and peaks at 8-16K, as the shape of the exposed work predicts; at one to four chunks OPCORD mostly keeps the original order and stays near the baseline
OPCORD shortens TTFT on all five LLMs, with outputs bit-identical to the original program's, by \PrefillGainLo--\PrefillGainHi$\times$ at prompts of 1{,}024 tokens and more (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}).
The gain rises with the prompt length and peaks at 8K or 16K tokens, where the overlap between the groups and the alternation within attention together are largest (Section~\ref{subsec:shape-eval}).
It dips where four attention columns already shorten attention, either in OPCORD's choice (Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-4B at 8K, Gemma3-4B at 32K) or in the baseline's (Llama-3.1-8B at 16K), which suggests that less of attention is left for the overlap to hide.
The two wide intervals, Llama-3.2-1B at 16K and Llama-3.1-8B at 32K tokens, each come from one slow timed run.
At prompts of one to four chunks, where the groups have little work to overlap, OPCORD composes dispatches at only one of the 20 cells, and its TTFT stays within \ShortGainLo--\ShortGainHi$\times$ of the baseline's.

\subsection{Sources of the Speedup}
\label{subsec:ablation}

% VALUE: at OPCORD's choices (order and split), nearly all of the prefill gain is the composer's reorder across dispatches: the stream pass changes nothing at four columns and finds no move at one, and at the default two-column split it contributes a within-operator share that grows with the prompt; packing alone contributes nothing; decode's gain needs the whole cycle renamed
Nearly all of the prefill gain comes from the new order of issue, first alternating attention's columns and then overlapping the column groups across dispatches, while the host time it hides matters only at short prompts.
We credit the prefill gain in one fixed order at the configuration OPCORD chooses, from the original order to the original order after the stream pass and then to the composed order.
The stream pass changes nothing on the four-column split, whose attention stream already issues each column's work before it waits, and finds no move on the one-column split.
On the default two-column split, by contrast, it gains \StreamSTwoLo--\StreamSTwoHi$\times$ at 16K tokens on the four LLMs whose attention waits between its columns.
The composer's reorder across dispatches carries the rest, \ComposerShareLo--\ComposerShareHi$\times$ at the configurations OPCORD chooses from 1K tokens, while packing alone gains nothing (Section~\ref{subsec:semantics}).
Of the composer's own saving, nearly all is overlap between the column groups, a median of \DecompOverlapShare\% across cells, and the rest the host and dispatch time it hides.
For Llama-3.2-1B at 4{,}096 tokens on the two-column split, which Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows, alternating attention's columns gives \DecompAlt$\times$, the overlap between the groups \DecompOverlap$\times$, and the hidden host time \DecompHost$\times$, or \DecompTotal$\times$ together in that batch.
Our runtime package issuing the original order runs within a few percent of the original program in either direction, and the baseline takes the faster of the two, so the gain comes from the order rather than from the package.
% SOURCE: at the picks the stream arm measures 1.000 at S4 and the pass makes no move at S1 (amd-npu s4-cells, s1-cells NOTES; vault decisions log, issue check). Gain decomposition on the 70 development cells (census workspace aie-cosched c4a8650, passes/results/2026-10-25-gain-decompose/decompose.json): orig / stream on S2 at 16,384 1.5045 (1B), 1.3581 (Qwen3-4B), 1.2791 (8B), 1.2324 (LFM2-1.2B), 1.0005 (Gemma3-4B); the overlap share of stream - comp, median 0.959; 1B 4096 S2 1.1879 x 1.421 x 1.0192 = 1.7203. Composer at the picks from 1,024 tokens, stream / comp at the pick's split where both were measured: 1.07 (Llama-3.1-8B 1,024, S2) to 1.63 (Llama-3.2-1B 16,384, S4) (figs/data/prefill-cells.json).

% VALUE: decode's gain needs the whole cycle renamed: keys and values alone gain nothing while the score buffer closes the cycle
In decode, renaming the keys and values alone, as FLM's authors' Gemma3 mapping does by hand~\citep{du2026gemma3}, is worth 0.3\%.
This is because the shared score buffer still closes the loop's critical cycle, as the critical-path test of Section~\ref{subsec:removal} requires.

\subsection{Choice of Order and Configuration}
\label{subsec:decision}

% VALUE: the event model's own choice of order and split stays within a few percent of the best measured option on every cell, and every cell where it misses falls in the near-tie band that a short measurement settles, whereas each fixed rule loses badly somewhere: never reordering gives up the long-prompt gains, always reordering loses at one or two chunks, a fixed split loses where the balance moves, and even the rule that reorders from four chunks at the baseline's split loses where the composed order's best split differs
The event model's own choice of order and configuration stays within \ChoiceOpcordMax\% of the measured minimum on every one of the \ChoiceCells{} cells and matches it on most (Figure~\ref{fig:choice}b).
These choices come from an earlier form of the event model with the same constants, and the accuracy below is that of the final form that Section~\ref{subsec:pricing} describes.
The final form changes \FinalChanged{} of the \ChoiceCells{} choices, and on the \FinalMeasured{} cells where its exact choice was measured, it loses at most \FinalLossMax\%, at Llama-3.2-1B's 1K-token prompt.
We compare its choice on every cell against the fastest order and configuration measured there, and against fixed rules.
Always reordering composes on the configuration that OPCORD chooses, always running $S_2$ takes the faster order on that configuration, and reordering from $C \ge 4$ chunks composes from 512 tokens on, on the baseline's configuration, and keeps the original order below.
Every cell where the choice misses the minimum falls inside the band in which the composer settles a near-tie by a short measurement (Section~\ref{subsec:pricing}), which these runs do not use.
Where the candidates of a cell ran in different batches, the comparison crosses batches, so a loss of a few percent lies within the drift between them.
Each fixed rule loses far more somewhere, since never reordering gives up the long prompts' gains, always reordering loses at one or two chunks (Section~\ref{subsec:shape-eval}), and a fixed configuration loses where the balance between the groups moves.
Reordering from four chunks, even on the baseline's configuration, loses up to \ChoiceFourBaseMax\% where the composed order runs faster on another one.

\subsection{Scope of the Condition}
\label{subsec:pays}
\label{subsec:clause}

% VALUE: the condition of Section 3.3, stated in full and tested: what rescheduling recovers is set by how a design cuts its work and by the prompt length; two tests, in the claim's order: exposure and bounds per design class across the census (the cut), the shape across prompt lengths (the length)
Rescheduling frees overlap only where the condition holds, in an amount set by how a design cuts its work and by the prompt length (Section~\ref{subsec:question}).
We show this across the design classes of our census and across prompt lengths, and test the first clause directly by breaking it.

\label{subsec:exposure}
% VALUE: the two measures per design: the exposure M (share of wall time in dispatches whose configuration holds two or more operators) and, for a closed stack, whose rewrites stay inside its dispatches, the joint bound B over the legal rewrites inside them; together they separate a design that exposes little work from one whose exposed work cannot overlap
We measure each design by two quantities.
Its \emph{exposure} $M$ is the share of the wall time spent in dispatches whose configuration holds two or more operators, since only such dispatches can overlap work inside them.
For a closed stack, whose rewrites stay inside its dispatches apart from the few of Section~\ref{subsec:closed}, $B$ is the bound of Section~\ref{subsec:removal} on all of its legal rewrites inside dispatches taken together.
The two imply a ceiling of $1 / \bigl((1 - M) + M / B\bigr)$ on the end-to-end gain from inside dispatches, which separates a design that exposes little work from one whose exposed work cannot overlap.

The prompt length sets the balance of the work the two groups can overlap, because attention's work per 128-token chunk grows with the chunk's position while the GEMMs' work per chunk stays fixed.
At the default configuration on every LLM, the reorder ties at one chunk, loses at two, and gains from four chunks to a peak of up to about $2\times$ at 8--16K tokens.
At two chunks there is little work to overlap, and every dispatch of the composed order starts only once the host has finished, which exposes the post-processing that the original order hides behind device work, although the costs at the dispatch boundaries fall.
