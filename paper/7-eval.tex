\subsection{Sources of the Gains}
\label{subsec:ablation}

% VALUE: at OPCORD's choices (order and split), nearly all of the prefill gain is the composer's reorder across commands: the stream pass changes nothing at four columns and finds no move at one, and at the default two-column split it contributes a within-operator share that grows with the prompt; packing alone contributes nothing; decode's gain needs the whole cycle renamed
At the splits OPCORD chooses for the composed order (Section~\ref{subsec:decision}), nearly all of the prefill gain comes from the composer's reorder across commands, because the stream pass changes nothing on the four-column split, whose attention stream already issues each column's work before it waits, and finds no move on the one-column split.
On the default two-column split, the stream pass alone gains \E{1.21--1.60}$\times$ at 16K tokens over the existing program and the composer adds its reorder across commands on top, whereas packing alone gains nothing (Section~\ref{subsec:semantics}).
% SOURCE: at the picks the stream arm measures 1.000 at S4 and the pass makes no move at S1 (amd-npu s4-cells, s1-cells NOTES; vault decisions log, issue check); stream-pass-only at S2 1.21-1.60x at 16K on three LLMs, a same-split figure, pre-final (development grids).

% VALUE: decode's gain needs the whole cycle renamed: keys and values alone gain nothing while the score buffer closes the cycle
In decode, renaming the keys and values alone, as a production mapping of another LLM does by hand~\citep{du2026gemma3}, is worth 0.3\%, because the shared score buffer still closes the loop's critical cycle, as the critical-path test of Section~\ref{subsec:removal} requires.

\subsection{Where Rescheduling Pays}
\label{subsec:pays}
\label{subsec:clause}

% VALUE: the condition of Section 3.3, stated in full and tested: what rescheduling recovers is set by how a design cuts its work and by the prompt length; four tests, in the claim's order: exposure and bounds per design class across the census (the cut), the shape across prompt lengths (the length), a synthetic design that varies the balance and the chunk count directly, and one control per clause
What rescheduling recovers is set by how a design cuts its work and by the prompt length, as the condition of Section~\ref{subsec:question} states, and we test that claim across the design classes of our census, across prompt lengths, on a synthetic design, and with one control per clause.

\label{subsec:exposure}
% VALUE: what the exposure measures and what it does not: per workload, the exposure M (share of wall time in commands whose configuration holds two or more operators), the bound b on reorders inside those commands, and the ceiling they imply; these cover the reorders inside a design's existing commands, which is all a closed stack lets us rewrite, whereas on an open fused design that issues one operator per command the composer re-cuts the commands, which the per-removal bound prices
How a design cuts its work into configurations, commands, and buffers sets how much of that work meets the condition.
For the reorders available inside a design's existing commands, which is all that a closed stack lets us rewrite, we state per workload its \emph{exposure} $M$, the share of the wall time spent in commands whose configuration holds two or more operators, together with the bound $b$ on the gain inside those commands (Section~\ref{subsec:removal}).
The two imply a ceiling on the end-to-end gain from inside commands of $1 / \bigl((1 - M) + M / b\bigr)$, which separates a design that exposes little work from one whose exposed work cannot overlap.
On an open fused design, OPCORD's composer also re-cuts the commands themselves, so that operators issued one per command come to share commands, a rewrite that the bound of each false order across commands covers, and on MLIR-AIR's prefill this re-cut carries nearly all of the gain (Section~\ref{subsec:ablation}).
% SOURCE: exposure metric and ceiling (aie-cosched registered 2a7990a; script 3d11670; rows a3ef131; partb/README).

\label{subsec:observed}
% VALUE: the census by design class, each placed by its two cuts, with what the condition predicts and what the bounds and measurements show: MLIR-AIR's prefill (fused configuration, one operator per command) gains, as Section 6.2 measures; FastFlowLM's flash prefill (fused, several operators per command) has M 0.82-0.87 but b 1.000 because its streams already keep every column busy, its waits between commands are mostly true, its one rewrite worth measuring (merging commands at the shortest prompts) is measured against its bound, and its decode pairs share an engine; IRON's modular operator sequences share no command and their streams price at one; the non-LLM designs overlap through dataflow; so every design the condition says can gain is one whose gain we measure
Across the 112 designs of our census, the condition predicts gains only where independent work in a fused configuration waits on other work in the CP's order or through a buffer it need not share, and the bounds and measurements bear each prediction out.
MLIR-AIR's chunked fused prefill holds every operator in one configuration but issues one command per operator and chunk, with attention and the GEMMs on disjoint column groups, so its column groups take turns on work that meets both clauses (Section~\ref{subsec:losses}), and the gains of Section~\ref{subsec:e2e} are measured there.
MLIR-AIR's fused decode keeps one buffer per role in its attention loop, so each block's transfer waits on the previous block's compute, and the loop pass's gain of Section~\ref{subsec:e2e} is measured there, whereas renaming only the keys and values, which leaves the score buffer closing the cycle, gains nothing (Section~\ref{subsec:ablation}).
FastFlowLM's prefill, taken as released, holds several operators per command and has an exposure $M$ of 0.82--0.87, yet the false orders inside its commands bound at $b = 1.000\times$, because its streams already keep every column busy at once, so deleting those orders lets no further work overlap.
Between its commands, most waits are true, since the host reads each command's output before it issues the next, and the false ones bound below $1.05\times$ with measured durations on every prompt we bound.
The one rewrite that its bound leaves worth measuring, which merges two of its commands at prompts of up to 128 tokens to save their dispatch, bounds at $1.075$--$1.093\times$ and saves \E{X}\% when replayed on the device.
Its decode runs one command per step, whose false pairs all share a core tile or a DMA channel, and bounds at $1.000$--$1.009\times$.
IRON's modular operator sequences give each operator its own configuration, so their operators share no command and pay reconfiguration between operators instead, and their 32 streams, which issue their transfers ahead of their waits, all bound at $1.000\times$.
Each of the non-LLM designs we read runs as one command whose stages already overlap through the array's own dataflow, and the 3 that IRON builds also bound at $1.000\times$.
Every design in the census that the condition says can gain is therefore one whose gain we measure.

\label{subsec:shape-eval}
% VALUE: the prompt length sets the balance of the exposed work: attention per chunk grows with the chunk's position while the GEMMs' work per chunk is fixed; at one split on every LLM the reorder ties at one chunk and loses at two (per-command costs and the pipeline's fill and drain outweigh the overlap), gains from four and rises to about twice at 8-16K where the groups balance, and falls at 32K where attention outweighs the GEMMs; in the middle range an estimate from the groups' busy times and the hidden host time tracks the gain, while only the event model prices the few-chunk costs and the incomplete overlap at long balanced prompts; the split moves the balance, so the best split changes with the length
The prompt length sets the balance of the work the two groups can overlap, since in a prefill cut into chunks of 128 tokens attention's work per chunk grows with the chunk's position while the GEMMs' work per chunk stays fixed, so overlapping the two column groups across chunks hides the shorter group's time behind the longer one's.
At one split on every LLM, the reorder ties at one chunk and loses at two, because the per-command costs and the time to fill and drain the pipeline of commands outweigh the overlap, and it gains from four chunks, rising to about $2\times$ at 8--16K tokens where the groups' work balances, before it falls at 32K, where attention outweighs the GEMMs.
From 1K to 4K tokens, an estimate from the two groups' busy times and the host time the overlap hides tracks the gain within 5\%, but only the event model, which composes each command, prices the dispatch costs at few chunks and the incomplete overlap at long balanced prompts.
The split moves this balance, so the split that minimizes TTFT also changes with the prompt length.
