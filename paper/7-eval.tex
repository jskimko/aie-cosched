\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

% VALUE: one setup paragraph: the platform and its power state, the software, the models (one per edge class), the workloads and prompt lengths, the baselines, how TTFT, decode, and turns are measured, the correctness gate, and how the machine state is controlled; defaults stated once
We measure on a GMKtec NucBox EVO-X2 with an AMD Ryzen\texttrademark{} AI Max+ 395 processor, whose XDNA~2 NPU has 8 columns of 4 compute tiles, and 64\,GB of shared system memory.
The host runs Linux~7.0 with the amdxdna driver and XRT~2.25.0~\citep{xdna-driver,xrt-repo}, NPU firmware 1.1.2.65 in its performance power mode, and the CPU's frequency governor and energy preference set to performance.
We evaluate one model per class of edge model: Llama-3.2-1B~\citep{meta2024llama32} and Qwen3-4B~\citep{yang2025qwen3} as dense models of two sizes, \E{Llama-3.1-8B as a dense 8B model, Gemma3-4B for attention within a sliding window, and LFM2-1.2B for layers that mix convolution and attention}, all with 4-bit quantized weights.
Prompts computed from scratch have 2{,}048 or 4{,}096 tokens, and appends add 512, 1{,}024, or 4{,}096 tokens to a cached context of 4{,}096, 16{,}384, or 28{,}672 tokens, followed by 250 generated tokens in an agent's turn.
These appends range from below the median to beyond the 90th percentile of the new tokens per step in coding-agent traces~\citep{zhu2026tracelab}, whereas the contexts stop at the 32{,}768-token window we build, below the traces' median prefix of 119K tokens, so our results cover agents whose context fits an edge model's window.
The designs are MLIR-AIR's fused prefill and fused decode~\citep{wang2026mlirair}, compiled with MLIR-AIE~\citep{mlir-aie-repo}.
The baselines are each design's own host program on its default split and, as a stronger baseline, the same program with its attention columns alternated, both with the same configuration, kernels, and weights as our schedules; \E{FastFlowLM~\citep{fastflowlm-repo} on the same weights gives an absolute reference.}
Every run's logits must be bit-identical to the baseline's, every append's logits bit-identical to those of a full prefill of the whole prompt, and the kernel log must show no driver or IOMMU message during the run.
TTFT is the wall time of the prefill call, from token identifiers to logits, decode time per token is measured over 250 greedy tokens after a prefill of the context, and a turn's time is its append, its 250 decoded tokens, and the handoff of the KV cache from the prefill design to the decode design.
We report the median of \E{5 timed runs of prefill and 3 of decode and turns} after one warm run, with the order of schedules rotated between rounds.
Each batch of runs holds the device exclusively, checks that no other process has it open, and brackets the runs with a DRAM bandwidth probe and an empty-command dispatch.
Because the aggregate DRAM read cap varies by up to 12\% between batches, every ratio compares runs within one batch.

\subsection{Synthetic Designs}
\label{subsec:synthetic}
% VALUE: three hand-written designs (not rewrites) test the all-or-nothing finding directly and fill the grid's two empty cells; each carries a certificate registered before it ran, that a removal off the binding cycle is worth nothing, and one certificate misses on the slow side at one work level, which is reported with its size
Three hand-written designs, which are not rewrites of shipped designs, test the finding of Section~\ref{sec:intro} and the two cells of Table~\ref{tab:grid} that hold no instance in the designs we rewrite.
Each registered, before it ran, that a removal our analysis places off the binding cycle is worth $1.00 \pm 0.01$.

The first puts one compute tile in a 2{,}048-iteration loop whose input and output each pass through an object FIFO, so that a FIFO of depth one closes a write-after-read cycle through the core and the two such edges lie on the same cycle.
At three levels of core work, no single removal gained more than 1.0\% and none changed the period by more than 1.8\% in either direction, the six measuring 1.010, 1.003, 1.000, 0.998, 0.982, and 0.988, while removing both gains $1.60\times$, $1.15\times$, and $1.03\times$.
The registered band held at two of the three levels and missed at the third, where both single removals came out marginally slower rather than unchanged, by 0.008 and 0.002.
The pair beat the better single by more than the band at every level.

The other two designs fill the empty cells, so neither is empty by construction.
For a name dependence in the CP's stream, two operators on separate columns are issued over eight chunks with one shared buffer, which makes each chunk's producer wait for the previous chunk's consumer; a two-entry ring removes that wait and gains 1.3\%, which clears its registered threshold by 0.003 but is smaller than the 1.5--2.0\% spread between repetitions, so it demonstrates the class rather than a gain worth taking.
For an order dependence inside a loop, a consumer holds its first acquire across a pair of elements, which is the hand edit of Section~\ref{subsec:ablation}; at buffer depth two that edge closes the producer--consumer cycle and removing it gains $2.00\times$, and at depth four, where the same edge lies off the binding cycle, it is worth 0.999.

\subsection{Distance to the Bound}
\label{subsec:bound-eval}
% VALUE: at each configuration's faster split the composer lies within 1.09-1.30x of the lower bound, whose binding term is a group's busy time rather than the CP; the bound is not strict where attention is extrapolated (16k + 4k)
\E{The gap to the lower bound per configuration, with its binding term.}

% VALUE: which group binds the bound flips with the workload, and that flip is what moves the faster split; the overlap cost of the bound appears only where the GEMMs bind; the rest of the distance to the bound is host work the schedule does not hide
Which group binds the lower bound flips with the workload.
From scratch at 2{,}048 tokens the GEMMs bind at every split on both models, whereas on an append to a 16{,}384-token context attention binds at every split.
The split names the number of attention columns, so a GEMM-bound configuration is served by fewer of them and an attention-bound one by more, which is why the faster split moves from two columns for prompts computed from scratch to four for appends.
The overlap cost of Section~\ref{subsec:removal} appears only where the GEMMs bind, at 1.0--5.1\% of TTFT, and accounts for part of the distance to the bound.
The remainder is the CP's time and the host work that the schedule does not hide.
On Llama-3.1-8B at 4{,}096 tokens, the composed prefill reads 51.5~GB/s averaged over its whole time including host work, which is 93--96\% of the cap, so on that model the schedule leaves little for any further reordering to win.

We formulated the bound after writing the passes, so the bounds reported here were computed before each gain was measured rather than before the passes were built.
No measured gain exceeded its bound in any of the 33 runs scored for that, including the 16 the registration had marked as the ones at risk, the closest reaching 1.023 of its bound.
On twelve cells measured after the bound was committed, the distance to it runs 1.17--1.63$\times$, and the model places each cell to within 0.03 at the median and 0.09 at the worst, always a little further from the bound than the schedule measures.
Our registered claim that the distance would stay within 1.30$\times$ therefore failed, on five of those twelve cells; the model had put all five above 1.30 before they ran, so the range was wrong exactly where the model said it would be, and we quote the narrower range only for the configurations it was computed on.
