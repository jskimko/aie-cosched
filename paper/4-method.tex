\section{OPCORD}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{subsec:overview}

% VALUE: removing these waits from compiled designs poses four challenges, each with its reason and the section that answers it: tell which of a design's many false orders can pay (the bound, Section 4, and the evidence of Section 6.7); recover the order from binaries without their source, although even an open design spreads it over the CP's stream, DMA chains, and core loops (Section 4); price an order without running it, because the artifact fixes the order but not the durations and the candidates multiply (Section 5); show a rewrite legal without the compiler that built the design (Sections 4 and 5)
Removing these waits from already compiled designs poses four challenges.
First, a design holds many false orders, most of which cost nothing, so each must be bounded to tell which kinds are worth a rewrite (Section~\ref{subsec:removal}).
Second, the order must be recovered from binaries without their source, and even an open design spreads it over the CP's stream, DMA chains, and core loops (Section~\ref{sec:order}).
Third, an order must be priced without running it, because the artifact fixes the order of the work but not its durations, and the candidates multiply with chunks, column splits, and removals (Section~\ref{subsec:pricing}).
Fourth, a reorder or a rename must be shown legal without the compiler that built the design, from what the instructions alone establish about buffers, locks, and routes (Sections~\ref{subsec:legality} and~\ref{subsec:checkers}).

% VALUE: design opener: OPCORD removes the false dependences of §3.3 in steps, each answering a property of the platform: a blocking in-order CP (reorder issue, by the composer across operators and the stream pass within one), one buffer per role in decode's loop (the loop pass's rings), an order and a split that pay differently with the prompt length (both chosen per length by the event model), designs it did not compile (checkers that refuse with a reason)
% DECISION: the opener follows the FPGA'27 design opener, each property of the platform followed by the mechanism that answers it, with one subsection per property (STYLE.md, Paper structure, Design; contract §6).
% DECISION: the legality conditions themselves live in Section~\ref{sec:order}; this section states only how OPCORD acts on them, namely checkers that accept a rewrite or refuse it with a reason (contract §6).
OPCORD runs the analysis of Section~\ref{sec:order} on each design, takes from it the segments and the legality conditions, provides a rewrite only for the kinds of false order whose bound leaves room, and answers each property of the platform behind the waits of Section~\ref{subsec:losses} with one mechanism.
Because a synchronization blocks the in-order CP, our composer and stream pass issue every group's work before the CP waits on any (Section~\ref{subsec:order}).
Because decode attention's loop keeps one buffer per role, our loop pass renames those buffers into rings (Section~\ref{subsec:rings}).
Because the issue order and the split that pay change with the prompt length, the composer chooses both per length with an event model (Sections~\ref{subsec:split} and~\ref{subsec:pricing}).
Because OPCORD rewrites designs it did not compile, checkers refuse, with a reason, any rewrite they cannot show legal (Section~\ref{subsec:checkers}).

\subsection{Reading Compiled Designs}
\label{sec:order}

% VALUE: the opening claim: nothing on XDNA 2 reorders work across engines, so a design's schedule and its waits can be read from the streams its stack emits, and the compiled design, open or closed, is the input to scheduling; arbitration is a resource and durations are left to measurement
% DECISION: the lead insight (the CP's instruction order is the array's schedule, fixed in the streams a toolchain emits) is stated in Sections 1 and 3; this opener states its consequence for the method; "rationed by false dependences" is never stated as the lead.
Because no hardware or runtime scheduler on XDNA~2 reorders work across engines, a design's schedule and every wait in it can be read from the instruction streams its stack emits, so we take a compiled design, open or closed, as the input to scheduling.
Arbitration on shared ports and locks, which serializes some work at run time, enters the analysis as a resource, and only durations, including the host's work between commands, are left to measurement.

% VALUE: the validity assumption, stated up front: every buffer a DMA shares with a core is touched by the core only between the lock operations its BDs name (the lockset discipline, cited; MLIR-AIE's object FIFOs guarantee it); every synchronization the CP and the DMAs take part in is in what we read, and cores reach host memory and memory tiles only through DMAs, so this suffices on XDNA 2; an access the analysis cannot observe is assumed to conflict
% SOURCE: spine v18, "The system", the principle and the validity assumption (vault paper-cosched-spine-v18).
The analysis rests on one assumption, that a core touches a buffer it shares with a DMA or a neighbouring core only between the lock operations that guard the buffer, which is the locking discipline that Eraser checks~\citep{savage1997eraser} and which MLIR-AIE's object FIFOs guarantee.
Because every synchronization in which the CP and the DMAs take part appears in the streams we read, and the cores reach host memory and memory tiles only through DMAs, this assumption suffices on XDNA~2, and any access the analysis cannot observe is treated as a conflict.
For a closed design, whose core programs we cannot read, the assumption cannot be checked, so the verdicts we give on closed designs rest on it.

\textbf{Reading a Stream.}
\label{subsec:reading}
\label{subsec:model}

% VALUE: the analysis represents a phase (one prefill or decode step) as operations on engines (CP instructions, BD executions, core loop iterations), with the host as one more agent whose steps read and write the bound buffers, each with read and write sets over locations that include every exclusively held resource, so a structural hazard is a conflict on a location (examples in appendix-body.tex, cut 2026-10-24); under static control, of the CP, the cores, and the host, one dispatch at given runtime parameters has one static set of operations; a loop with data-dependent control is reported and left out, and every loop of the designs we rewrite is analysed; durations and host work are measured inputs (opening paragraph)
The analysis represents a \emph{phase}, which is the sequence of commands that the host issues for one prefill or one decode step, as operations on engines, namely one per instruction of a command's stream on the CP, one per execution of a BD on a DMA channel, and one per iteration of a core's counted loop, each with a set of locations that it reads and a set that it writes.
Locations cover every resource that one operation holds exclusively, from memory bytes to BD slots, locks, DMA channels, task-queue slots, switch ports, and configuration registers, so a structural hazard is also a conflict on a location.
These engines and the host are the \emph{agents} of the analysis, and the host's steps between commands read and write the buffers that the commands bind, taken from the host program where we have it, observed at the runtime's boundary where we do not (Section~\ref{subsec:closed}), and assumed to conflict with everything otherwise.
Under static control, the CP's stream is straight-line, every core loop's trip count depends on the phase's runtime parameters rather than on computed data, and the host's order of commands depends only on the LLM and the lengths of the context and the prompt, so a phase has one static set of operations.
A loop with data-dependent control is reported and left out, whereas every loop of the MLIR-AIR designs we rewrite is analysed (Section~\ref{subsec:census}).
% SOURCE: operations, locations, and static control (vault lab-cosched-passes (30), item 1); every loop analysed: on all nine fused decodes (1B, 8B, Gemma3, LFM2, Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-4-mini, Qwen2.5-7B / 3B, Qwen3-8B) 0 loops refused (aie-cosched results/2026-10-23-phase-rerun, registered 7687182; mlir-aie dev line 0c5e8e37fac, 6b9e6666991, lit 46/46), and all 3,488 MLIR-AIR prefill loops judged (decisions log, loop census part 3; mlir-aie ba266793aa8); the host's static control (outline v9 section 1); the decode kernels' early return on the pad block branches on a runtime parameter, so it stays static per context length (lab-decode-long-context (10)). Nested-control mechanics: appendix-body.tex, app:nested (cut 2026-10-24).

% VALUE: happens-before is completion before start, not issue order; each direct edge comes from one of 11 mechanisms at the stream, data-movement, or loop granularity and is attributed to exactly one (the attribution choices, edges per gated operation, and uncovered operations in appendix-body.tex, cut 2026-10-24); a segment is a run of synchronizations with the issue they gate; the evaluation checks the reading
From these operations, the analysis builds a \emph{happens-before} graph, the dependence graph over every agent, in which an edge from $a$ to $b$ means that $b$ cannot start until $a$ has completed.
Issue order alone does not create such an edge, because a queue push is issued in order but its transfer completes later.
Each direct edge comes from exactly one of 11 ordering mechanisms, such as a synchronization, a BD chain, or a lock, at the granularity of the CP's stream, of data movement, or of a core loop, and Section~\ref{subsec:census} checks this reading against what each stack dispatches.
An operator's stream is a sequence of \emph{segments}, where each segment is a run of synchronizations followed by the issue instructions they gate and the first segment has none.
% SOURCE: the mechanism table, attribution order, granularities, UNATTRIBUTED, and issue edges (vault lab-cosched-passes (30), item 2); the library AIECoschedDeps (mlir-aie f1725dae1f1, pinned); segments as in txn.segments (amd-npu a0ee2ed prefill-overlap/txn.py) and the formulation's operators (Section~\ref{subsec:formulation}); one edge per (wait, gated op) pair (lab-cosched-passes (30), item 3).

% VALUE: each direct edge is classed by the pairs of operations it alone orders: true if one is read after write, name if one is write after read or write, order otherwise, after Bernstein's conditions and compilers' anti and output dependences; renaming removes a name dependence, reordering an order dependence, a true one stays; loop-carried edges are classed alike (redundant and control counts in appendix-body.tex, cut 2026-10-24); a class assumes every other edge in place, which is why a rewrite is judged as a whole
For each direct edge, the analysis computes the pairs of operations that the edge alone orders, namely those ordered with it and not without it.
The edge is a true dependence if one of these pairs reads a location after the other writes it, a name dependence if none does but one writes after the other reads or writes, and an order dependence otherwise.
Loop-carried edges are classed in the same way on the loop's iterations.
Because each class is computed with every other edge in place, it says what removing that one edge could free, not whether a rewrite that removes several is legal (Section~\ref{subsec:legality}).
% SOURCE: classification by Lost(e), REDUNDANT, CONTROL, loop-carried distance (vault lab-cosched-passes (30), item 3); computed at stream granularity by aie-cosched-stream-deps (mlir-aie 2392abdc230 onward, fixture stream-turns with ORDER, NAME, RAW and REDUNDANT edges, lab-cosched-passes (49)), which agrees with its Python oracle on every compared count on 792 of 793 population streams, the 793rd too large for either (mlir-aie 0489d325082, lab-cosched-passes (53)); at loop granularity by AIECoschedDeps, whose buffer classes are the Lost-pair classes of the loop-carried edges (lab-cosched-passes (33)), with order edges and critical cycles computed in results/2026-10-23-order-loop (mlir-aie dev 58569749d3e..36f725e8e6e).
% DECISION: the classes, the unattributed and redundant counts, and the pairs each edge alone orders are computed by the analysis (the stream graph and the loop library); the stream-granularity instances below were identified by reading the designs and confirmed by measurement, so the text never says that the analysis found them. The census of Section~\ref{subsec:census} counts instances that the stream pass proposes and the device confirms; it never classifies edges, and the two quantities are never conflated in the text.

\textbf{Closed Binaries.}
\label{subsec:closed}

% VALUE: a closed stack ships binaries only, so its streams are recorded at the runtime's boundary and lifted with a byte-identical round trip; closed streams are read and judged (each issue attributed by the decoded routes) but not rewritten, and removals are made on the open reimplementation of the same designs
FastFlowLM ships its designs as binaries, so we record their instruction streams at the runtime's boundary and lift each into MLIR-AIE's runtime sequence, its intermediate form for a command's instruction stream, from which every stream recorded offline re-emits byte for byte.
We read, judge, and bound these streams, attributing each issue to a column group by the decoded routes, and we observe the host at the same boundary, through the buffers each command binds and the engine's own reads and writes of mapped buffers between commands.
On a closed stack, a rewrite could only swap a command's stream, reorder or merge commands that no closed host step separates, or narrow a wait, letting a host step start before the command it waits on has completed once the results it reads are ready, where every host access to device results is observed at the runtime's boundary, so the closed host's own steps and its core loops stay as they are.
We therefore bound every such rewrite (Section~\ref{subsec:removal}) and replay on the device only those whose bound leaves room, to test the bound against measurement rather than to deploy the rewrite, whereas OPCORD rewrites the open designs in use (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
% SOURCE: the closed-stack rule and the host observed at the driver boundary (spine v18, The system; census partb/README); the measured rewrites are the merges at the 128-token bucket and e4898ce3's stream (census partb/replay/, registered 3ae0370, 67d54fe).
% SOURCE: recording libhrx (FastFlowLM 44d01787, gemma4e-flash), every prompt length 1-1,024 recorded with 0 /dev/accel opens; 8 buckets, 218 distinct streams, round trip and identity 218 / 218 (vault lab-cosched-passes (14)); lifter fix mlir-aie 91f64387ffb, 375 / 375 MLIR-AIR streams byte-identical (lab-cosched-passes (3)).
% SOURCE: streams recorded with a library that never opens the device, or captured with an XRT interposer during a device run (gemma4e-flash:e2b, 1,009 tokens; appendix-body.tex, app:capture (cut 2026-10-24)); route maps from the cdo_routes decoder: shim 3 and shim 6 MM2S0 reach only columns 0 and 7 (vault lab-flm-streams (11), (13)); the attribution rule and both labels are in appendix-body.tex, app:attribution (cut 2026-10-24).

\textbf{Bounding a Removal.}
\label{subsec:removal}

% VALUE: one removal is worth at most the overlap it blocks on typed resources, given every other edge; zero off the longest path; in a loop the maximum cycle mean governs; Amdahl's law for an ordering, from the order and measured durations, not a trace
Removing a false order $d$ speeds a phase up by at most $T / (T - O(d))$, where $T$ is the phase's time and $O(d)$ is the time during which $d$ keeps work on disjoint resources from overlapping, given every other edge of the happens-before graph.
The resources are typed, namely the column groups, the CP with its cost at each command boundary, the DRAM read rate up to its cap, and the DMA channels that one group uses on another group's columns, and each runs its own work in turn, so the phase's time is the longest path through the graph with those resources' orders included, and $O(d)$ is zero when $d$ lies off it.
Because these resources include the core tiles and DMA channels that a rewrite cannot duplicate, and a true dependence stays an edge, a false order that fails either clause of the condition (Section~\ref{subsec:question}) frees no overlap and bounds at $1.000\times$.
In a loop, the maximum cycle mean of the repeating graph determines the time per iteration instead~\citep{ghamarian2006throughput}.
The bound is thus Amdahl's law applied to an ordering, computed from the program and from \emph{probes}, which time each operator dispatched alone, without the timing trace that the tools of Section~\ref{sec:related} start from.

% VALUE: for a set of removals the textbook critical-path condition applies (cited, one sentence, never a finding); we use it as a test that discards sets that cannot pay; on this machine it is necessary, not sufficient (the next longest path; shared capacity); the decode instance is in the evaluation
A set of removals gains only if it cuts every path or cycle that attains the maximum, the critical-path rule of interaction-cost analysis~\citep{fields2003interaction} that Section~\ref{subsec:question} refines, and then only down to the next longest path and to what the shared DRAM read cap leaves.
We use it as a test on the happens-before graph with measured costs, which discards every candidate set that cannot pay.
To bound a set of removals taken at once, as Section~\ref{subsec:pays} does for the census, we delete them together, the false edges as edges and the rewrites inside commands and the merges as shorter durations, and recompute the longest path with every command that shares a column or a channel kept in its recorded order, so the joint bound is $T$ over that path's length, a bound over the rewrites that keep each shared resource's order.
% SOURCE: joint bound b = T0 / Tj, both by the same fixed-order schedule (aie-cosched passes/census_partb_terms.py, schedule() line 150, legal-rewrite-space joint in main() 323-350, last change 55e5c6e); false boundary edges deleted, accepted in-command savings and merges as shorter durations, true waits narrowed; recorded order kept on shared resources (pass-0, 2026-10-25).
% SOURCE: the acyclic condition is the minimum cut of a critical subnetwork in project crashing (Kelley 1961; Fulkerson 1961), the cyclic one the iteration bound and maximum cycle mean (Ghamarian 2006; Stuijk 2008), and the set view is interaction cost (Fields 2003); all are cited in Section 7, never claimed.
% SOURCE: the test is the in-tree set test, acyclic half (longest path, cut, next longest; mlir-aie 7240529eb562, lit 19/19) and loop half (periodic graph, Howard's algorithm, critical subgraph; mlir-aie 54a8b8e192c, lit 10/10) (vault paper-cosched-decisions-log, P1 and P3).
% DECISION: the critical-path condition is textbook; Section 3.3 states it as the rule the condition refines and this section uses it as a test, each with its citation; it is a tool, never a finding, and the decode partial-rename instance lives in Section 6.3 (section contract; outline v10.1).
% DECISION: the condition is stated as necessary only: a longest-path or cycle criterion is valid on an uncapacitated graph, and this machine has a shared DRAM cap, a shared command processor and a per-command boundary cost; the capacity half is the measured regime of this section, and the 8B at the cap is the negative instance inside the paper.
% DECISION: the bound's sentences follow outline v9 section 3's rows (P4 withdraws them, P4u marks them untested); the critical-cycle sentences follow the W1-W5 table.

% VALUE: the bound is prescriptive (build a removal only where its bound exceeds its cost); since it never falls below one it cannot predict a loss, which the event model prices instead (the short prompts, in the evaluation)
A kind of removal is therefore worth a rewrite only where its bound exceeds its cost, which for a rename is the local memory, BDs, locks, and channels it takes, and for a reorder the synchronization and planning it adds.
The bound cannot predict a loss, such as the per-command costs that make reordering lose on prompts of two chunks, so OPCORD's event model prices those (Section~\ref{subsec:pricing}).
% SOURCE: the bound and its typed resources (outline v9 section 2, C1; vault lab-bound-validation (1)).

\subsection{Legality}
\label{subsec:legality}

% VALUE: a class does not make a rewrite safe, because a rewrite moves many instructions at once, so each rewrite is judged as a whole against conditions that the streams and their configuration decide without the source: a reorder keeps every instruction once and each operator's and each channel's order, and every pair it inverts must be independent on host bytes, BDs, task queues, ports, and its own group's fences; between whole operators, disjoint device state makes every interleaving legal at once; operators on one group stay in sequence
% DECISION: classes are the report and legality is the checker's; no edge is removed on the strength of its class alone (vault lab-cosched-passes (30), item 3).
A rewrite moves many instructions at once, so a class alone does not make it safe, and we judge each rewrite as a whole against conditions that the streams and their configuration decide without the design's source.
A reorder keeps every instruction exactly once and keeps each operator's segments and each channel's pushes in their own order.
Of each pair of instructions that it inverts, neither may write host bytes that the other reads or writes, neither may cross a lock, runtime-parameter, or configuration write of its own column group, and the two may share no stream-switch port.
No BD may be rewritten while a transfer that loaded it may still be queued, no push may move above the write of its own BD, and no reorder may leave a channel's task queue full while every transfer queued on it waits on work the CP has yet to issue, which would stall the CP for good.
The checkers decide this last condition by replaying each channel's queue through the original's rounds of issue, letting a transfer retire only once the inputs of its round are issued, coupling the channels whose routes share a tile, and allowing no buffering ahead, which errs toward refusal.
Between two whole operators, a stronger condition, that neither can disturb the other's state on the device, makes every interleaving of their segments legal at once.
It holds when each operator's register writes address only its own group's columns and touch only shim BDs and DMA queues, its own cores' locks, and runtime parameters in its own cores' memory, and when the two patch disjoint argument slots or disjoint byte ranges of one slot's buffer and neither writes a buffer that the other reads or writes.
Operators on the same group are composed in sequence instead, so their writes to that group's registers keep the order of separate commands.
% SOURCE: the composer's conditions: each check turns red on a defeated input (swapped groups, an unstripped stream, a synchronization before an issue op, a patch onto another group's argument, a duplicated column, a reordered or dropped segment) (lab-prefill-overlap (3), (4)); txn.compose asserts each stream's segment order, every op kept once, and equal headers (amd-npu a0ee2ed prefill-overlap/txn.py).
% SOURCE: the stream checker on every inverted pair: ranges (RANGE-OVERLAP), BDs (BD-REUSE, PUSH-BEFORE-BD-WRITE, QUEUE-OVERFLOW at the depth of mechanism 3), order invariants (NOT-PERMUTATION, CHANNEL-ORDER, BD-ORDER, SKELETON-MOVED), routes (ROUTE-1-PORT, ROUTE-SHIM-LOCK), and fences of the same group (FENCE-CROSSED for lock, switch, core, and LOADPDI writes; FENCE-CROSSED-UNPROVEN for its runtime-parameter words) (legality.py's pairwise inversion rule, vault lab-cosched-passes (10), (14), (36); the C++ checker inside aie-cosched-schedule, P6 closed, lab-cosched-passes (57), cosched-flag b46744f63c6).

% VALUE: a rename is legal only for a location whose every conflict across uses is WAR or WAW, so carried state stays, and only with a free added instance: no slot reused before its previous reader completes; in a loop, one lock pair whose protocol the added slot extends, within the tile's BDs and local memory; a condition the streams cannot decide refuses the rewrite with that condition; the conditions hold under the execution semantics and are not claimed sufficient for every design, and every rewrite run is also bit-identical
% DECISION: the conditions are stated as what the streams and their configuration can check, never as sufficient for every design (introduction).
A rename is legal only for a location whose conflicts across its uses are all write-after-read or write-after-write, so state carried from one iteration or chunk to the next stays in place.
Its added instance must also be free, with no slot reused before its previous reader has completed, and in a loop the buffer must be guarded by one pair of locks, whose protocol the added slot extends, with the slot fitting in the tile's BDs and local memory.
Where the streams cannot decide a condition, such as a buffer range they do not fix, the rewrite is refused with that condition as its reason.
Because the conditions hold only if the locations cover all the state an operation touches, we do not claim that they suffice for every design, and every rewrite we run also leaves the outputs bit-identical (Section~\ref{subsec:setup}).
% SOURCE: the loop conditions: the class rule renames only buffers whose every cross-iteration conflict is WAR / WAW and leaves RAW-carried state untouched; one free / full lock pair, the BD and local-memory budgets (16 BDs, 64 KiB), checked on the input and re-derived on the output (lab-cosched-passes (33), (57)); the ring condition: no slot reused before its previous reader has completed (merged.colour and assert_no_hazard, amd-npu a0ee2ed; the hazard check turns red on an overlapping slot, lab-prefill-overlap (9)).
% SOURCE: refusals where the stream cannot decide: RANGE-UNKNOWN, RESOURCE-UNPROVEN, FENCE-CROSSED-UNPROVEN (legality.py codes, lab-cosched-passes (10)); in the census, 16 production-engine streams refused with QUEUE-OVERFLOW, 8 of them also FENCE-CROSSED-UNPROVEN, and IRON's proposals refused for queue room not provable from the stream (aie-cosched baf37e0, lab-cosched-passes (62), P7b; B-prime, (46)).
% SOURCE: bit-identical logits for every composed prefill and append, and identical tokens and logits for every renamed decode (Section~\ref{subsec:setup} defaults; vault lab-decode-long-context (22)).

\textbf{Checkers.}
\label{subsec:checkers}

% VALUE: the composer and both passes each carry a checker that tests the legality conditions of Section 4 (pointed to, not restated) on the facts its rewrite touches; the composer's checks stop the composition rather than drop an operator; the stream pass checks every inverted pair and leaves the stream unchanged on a finding, saying whether illegal or not shown legal; the loop pass refuses a buffer it cannot show renamable and confirms its own output
The composer and both passes each carry a checker that tests the conditions of Section~\ref{subsec:legality} on the facts its rewrite touches, and a check that fails leaves the composition or the stream unchanged and names its reason, rather than dropping an operator or an instruction.
The stream pass's checker also has a second, independent implementation, against which Section~\ref{subsec:legality-eval} compares its verdicts.
The loop pass also refuses a buffer used outside its block loop, and it re-checks each ring and the carried state on its output.

A hand edit reaches only the streams someone reads, whereas a pass that accepts or refuses every stream applies its legality condition to designs nobody has read, whose verdicts Section~\ref{subsec:census} reports as judgments rather than gains.
